The kitchen

A Japanese table that happens
to be entirely plants.

Ruma means house — and that is exactly what this is: a small, air-conditioned room in Ruko Nico Residence where Japanese food is cooked without a single piece of fish or meat. Sushi, ramen, rice bowls, gyoza and yakitori, made from tofu, mushrooms, vegetables and rice.

The idea is not to imitate. It is to build the flavours properly: a curry simmered until it is fragrant, tofu pockets seasoned by hand, katsu crumbed and fried to order. Guests keep writing about the same two things — the portions are bigger than expected, and the bill is smaller than expected.

It has become the kind of place Batam food people recommend to each other: for the plant-based friends, for the gym crowd, and for anyone who simply wants a good bowl of ramen at a fair price.

The menu

What people come back for.

Four sections, everything plant-based. Items marked new and most ordered are the ones guests write about.

Inari Sushi Rolls Signature

Seasoned tofu pockets packed with sushi rice. One reviewer's verdict: “simple, clean, and very satisfying.”

Inari Chuka Wakame Signature

Sweet fried tofu skin over seasoned seaweed salad, finished with a sliver of chilli for lift.

Mentai Roll

Maki rolls finished with a creamy plant-based mentai sauce, sesame and a little heat.

Katsu Roll

Crumbed and fried plant-based katsu rolled with rice and nori — crisp, then soft.

Avocado Maki

Cool avocado, sushi rice and toasted sesame. The quiet one on the platter that always empties first.

Tamago Add-on

Sweet rolled omelette, sliced to order. The add-on regulars order without being asked.

Karai Yakiniku Ramen New

A spicy, deeply savoury broth with grilled plant-based yakiniku. The newest bowl on the list.

Cheezy Ramen New

Creamy, cheesy, unreasonably comforting. Built for the days Batam rain does not stop.

House Ramen

The everyday bowl — clear, savoury broth, springy noodles, vegetables and a soft egg.

Omurice Hambagu Mentai

Fluffy omelette over rice, a mushroom hambagu, and a Japanese curry sauce guests describe as “very fragrant.”

Yakimeshi Katsu Curry

Fried rice under crisp katsu, flooded with glossy Japanese curry.

Ricebowl Katsu

Breaded katsu over rice with house sauce and a heap of shredded cabbage.

Plant-Based Chicken Curry

Fragrant Japanese curry, plant-based chicken, plain rice — the combination reviewers keep describing.

Plant-Based Chicken Mentai

Creamy mentai sauce over plant-based chicken and rice. Rich, mild, hard to stop eating.

Yakitori Signature

Glazed skewers with sesame — “perfectly glazed and tender,” with the sauce balanced sweet against savoury.

Gyoza

Pan-fried dumplings with a chilli-soy dip. Order two plates; one never survives the table.

Chuka Wakame

Seasoned seaweed salad — cool, sesame-forward, and the right thing before a bowl of curry.

Why plant-based

Japanese technique,
grown ingredients.

The broth is built from kombu and mushrooms instead of bonito. The katsu is crumbed around tofu and gluten, not pork. What stays is the part that matters: the glaze, the char, the balance of sweet, salt and heat.
